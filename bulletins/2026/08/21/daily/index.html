Hong Kong


🟡 Mitigating risks in receiving deposits through simplified eDDA arrangements 🇭🇰
Item ID: item-20260820-sfc-circulars-26EC51
⏰ Deadline: No source deadline stated
Products: eDDA, client deposits, virtual assets

The SFC’s 20 August circular addresses licensed corporations, SFC-licensed virtual-asset service providers and associated entities receiving client deposits through payee-initiated simplified eDDA. It highlights unauthorised, erroneous and fraudulent setup or debit risks, including indemnity exposure, and expects firms to assess the arrangement, reasonably ascertain account-owner authorisation, verify identity information and respond appropriately to unmet safeguards or red flags. The circular reinforces underlying internal-control, AML/CFT and notification obligations, while its examples do not prescribe a single control design. It may affect a banking group only where an in-scope securities or virtual-asset entity uses this arrangement, rather than ordinary bank eDDA; management should confirm that perimeter and evidence the risk assessment, checks, red-flag handling and client settings or consent.

Source: Circular to licensed corporations, SFC-licensed virtual asset service providers and associated entities — Mitigating risks in receiving deposits through simplified eDDA arrangements


🟡 Implementation guidance on Banking (Capital) Rules 🇭🇰
Item ID: item-20260820-hkma-brdr-35ab6b0e37
⏰ Deadline: No source deadline stated
Products: lending, capital reporting

HKMA’s BRDR lists a current circular, issued on 20 August 2026, titled “Implementation guidance on Banking (Capital) Rules” and addressed to all authorized institutions. The listing identifies non-securitisation credit-risk capital adequacy and credit-risk management, with annexes headed Chapter III (STC approach) and Chapter IV (IRB approach). The available evidence supports a prudential applicability review, but not a conclusion on a changed capital treatment, threshold, transition, reporting date or specific obligation because the circular and annex bodies were not extracted. Prudential reporting and credit-risk owners should obtain the authenticated documents and confirm their status, applicability and any dates before mapping any material effects on capital calculations, lending economics, credit data/models, policy or returns.

Source: Implementation guidance on Banking (Capital) Rules


🟡 Insurance Authority observations on premium-financing facilities 🇭🇰
Item ID: item-20260820-hkma-brdr-8934644ea0
⏰ Deadline: No source deadline stated
Products: premium finance, insurance distribution

HKMA’s BRDR lists a current circular issued on 20 August 2026 to all authorized institutions under Wealth Management & MPF—Insurance products. Its title concerns Insurance Authority observations on insurance business carried on using premium-financing facilities, and the listing indicates that a separate Insurance Authority circular is enclosed. This supports screening entities that provide premium finance or distribute relevant insurance products, but the available evidence does not disclose the observations, any expectation or finding, affected transaction types, deadline or change from the prior position. Wealth, insurance-distribution and lending owners should obtain the authenticated HKMA and Insurance Authority documents, inventory relevant exposures and make an applicability decision before considering product approval, sales/disclosure, credit underwriting, servicing or complaints evidence.

Source: Circular issued by the Insurance Authority regarding observations on insurance business carried on with the use of premium financing facilities


🟡 First live tokenised-deposit transaction on Swift’s blockchain-based ledger 🇭🇰
Item ID: item-20260820-hk-banks-news-c040ad9fb8
⏰ Deadline: No source deadline stated
Products: tokenised deposits, cross-border payments, corporate liquidity

HSBC and Standard Chartered announced a live bank-to-bank cross-border tokenised-deposit transaction using Swift’s blockchain-based ledger. The announcement says payment messages were exchanged through the ledger, with obligations recorded, matched and netted on each bank’s infrastructure before final settlement through existing systems; HSBC says its Tokenised Deposit Service is live in Hong Kong and five other markets. This is a participating-bank market announcement, not a Hong Kong regulatory instrument: it establishes neither a customer rollout nor a new Hong Kong obligation, approval or deadline. It signals potential relevance for corporate cross-border liquidity and payment propositions, while transaction scale, client participation, legal structure, regulatory engagement and control design remain unstated; comparable initiatives should be assessed for settlement, reconciliation, AML/CFT, data, availability and dependency questions.

Source: HSBC and Standard Chartered execute first live tokenised deposit transaction on Swift’s blockchain-based ledger
