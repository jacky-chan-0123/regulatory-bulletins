Executive Summary

• 🟡 Hong Kong’s first-phase licensed-money-lender measures are now effective, with a 1 June 2027 Credit Data Smart reporting and access change for unsecured-personal-loan lenders. They do not establish a new obligation for authorized institutions on the available evidence, but warrant a targeted check of lending-market, referral and credit-data dependencies.
• 🟡 HKCERT reports active exploitation of one Apache Tomcat vulnerability under a specified non-default configuration. Internet-facing payments, digital-banking and SME-onboarding services should establish whether affected versions and the configuration are present.
• 🟡 The FCA has finalised UK transaction-reporting changes effective 3 April 2028. They are not Hong Kong requirements, but are a useful control and delivery benchmark for UK-regulated reporting entities and comparable reporting architecture.
• 🟢 INTERPOL’s Africa-wide assessment identifies AI-enabled scam, credential-harvesting and identity-bypass mechanisms as a fraud-control watch point. It is non-binding and does not evidence Hong Kong prevalence or a Hong Kong incident.

Hong Kong


🟡 Licensed money-lender measures now effective 🇭🇰
Item ID: item-20260801-fstb-1ce0dd213a

⏰ Deadline / products: Effective 1 August 2026; Credit Data Smart change from 1 June 2027. Licensed money lenders; unsecured personal lending and credit data.

The Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau says the first-phase measures prohibit licensed money lenders from requesting loan referees. They also cap debt servicing for unsecured personal loans at 35% for monthly income at or below HK$6,000 and 40% for monthly income of HK$6,001–12,000. From 1 June 2027, unsecured-personal-loan lenders must regularly submit borrower credit information to Credit Data Smart and meet specified conditions to access its data for application assessment. This announcement concerns licensed money lenders and does not, on the available evidence, establish a new obligation for Hong Kong authorized institutions; lending, credit-data and customer-conduct teams may nevertheless need to assess referral, competitive and shared-data dependencies. The underlying instrument, exemptions, transition arrangements and Credit Data Smart entry conditions are not set out in the release.

Source: First-phase measures to enhance regulation of licensed money lenders take effect today


🟡 Apache Tomcat vulnerabilities; one under active exploitation 🇭🇰
Item ID: item-20260805-hkcert-4a6e635770

Deadline / products: No source deadline stated. Digital banking, payments and SME-onboarding applications.

HKCERT lists affected Apache Tomcat releases 9.0.13–9.0.116, 10.1.0-M1–10.1.53 and 11.0.0-M1–11.0.20, with fixes in 9.0.117, 10.1.54 and 11.0.21. It says exploitation may cause security-restriction bypass, information disclosure or data manipulation, and identifies CVE-2026-34486 as being exploited in the wild. Under the specified non-default EncryptInterceptor configuration, that vulnerability could disclose sensitive information. HKCERT’s bulletin is technical guidance rather than a binding rule or an identified bank incident; the immediate implementation question is whether publicly reachable applications or suppliers use an affected version and configuration. The source does not establish an affected institutional footprint, compromise, loss or control failure.

Source: Apache Tomcat Multiple Vulnerabilities

🟡 FCA finalises transaction-reporting changes 🇬🇧
Item ID: item-20260803-fca-benchmark-transaction-reporting

⏰ Deadline / products: Effective 3 April 2028. UK transaction reporting, investment activity and reporting technology.

The UK FCA has finalised rules that reduce reporting fields from 65 to 52, remove FX derivatives and seven million instruments traded only on EU venues from scope, and shorten the historical-error correction period from five to three years. The FCA estimates annual savings above £100 million while retaining an expectation for accurate, high-quality data. Prepared firms may make certain changes sooner under flexible supervision, but the press release does not establish the detailed scope, transition or early-change eligibility; it identifies PS26/15 as the governing detail. The rules affect UK-regulated reporting firms, their reporting platforms, reference data, reconciliations and outsourced-reporting arrangements, not Hong Kong reporting obligations. For comparable Hong Kong securities activity, the control benchmark is a governed review of reportable populations and data lineage rather than deregulation.

Source: FCA finalises rules to cut firms' transaction reporting costs over £100m a year

🟢 INTERPOL assessment flags AI-enabled cybercrime mechanisms 🇺🇳
Item ID: item-20260803-interpol-financial-cyber-9491bd3cc1

Deadline / products: No source deadline stated. Payments, digital services and SME onboarding.

INTERPOL’s 3 August Africa assessment, based on survey data from 36 member countries, reports AI linked to 55% of reported cybercrime. It reports regional cybercrime-related losses rising from USD192 million in 2024 to USD484 million, driven primarily by AI-facilitated scams, credential harvesting and automated social engineering. The assessment also identifies synthetic identities, biometric-bypass attempts and a lack of real-time information sharing among banks, telecoms and law enforcement as fraud vulnerabilities. This is non-binding, Africa-specific institutional research, not a Hong Kong incident, finding or obligation; the regional figures are not a bank loss, fine or enforcement amount. Its value locally is as a monitoring benchmark for identity proofing, payment-scam detection and intervention against business-email-compromise and social-engineering tactics, while Hong Kong prevalence and lawful information-sharing options remain unknown.

Source: INTERPOL report finds AI linked to more than half of cybercrime in Africa
