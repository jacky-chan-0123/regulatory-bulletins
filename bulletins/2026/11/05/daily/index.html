Hong Kong


🔴 Government welcomes passage of Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework and amended Common Reporting Standard Bill 🇭🇰
Item ID: item-20261104-daily-exploratory-search-36149d91eb
⏰ Deadline: CARF information collection in 2027; exchanges from 2028; amended CRS takes effect in 2028 and exchanges start in 2029
Products: Digital assets, deposits, investments

The Government announced that the Legislative Council passed the Bill to implement the OECD Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF) and amended Common Reporting Standard (CRS). It states that relevant service providers will collect CARF-specified information in 2027 for Inland Revenue Department exchanges with partner jurisdictions from 2028; amended CRS takes effect in 2028, with exchanges from 2029. The announcement says the exchanges will be reciprocal and subject to data confidentiality and security, and that the IRD will issue guidance and provide technical support. The announcement does not set out covered providers or accounts, exemptions, reporting format, due-diligence rules or commencement mechanics, so applicability should remain subject to enacted text and IRD guidance.

Actions: In the next 30 days, Tax Reporting and Compliance should assign accountability, document an applicability decision, and inventory relevant entities, products and data. Then map CRS and crypto-asset data lineage, vendor dependencies and customer communications, with second-line challenge, before designing configuration or testing against operative requirements and IRD guidance.

Source: Government welcomes passage of Inland Revenue (Amendment) (Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework and Amended Common Reporting Standard) Bill 2026


🟡 Policy on the development of digital assets 🇭🇰
Item ID: item-20261104-fstb-ec4be02ffb
⏰ Deadline: Government intends to introduce a Bill by end-2026; no commencement or compliance date stated
Products: Digital assets, investments, payments

In a Legislative Council reply, the Government said it and the Securities and Futures Commission will introduce a Bill by end-2026 to amend the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance and extend the virtual-asset trading-platform regime to dealing, advisory, management and custody services. The reply also says the Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau and HKMA are advancing a legislative review on wider distributed-ledger-technology use in fixed income and digital assets. It describes a risk-based, technology-neutral approach, with standards aligned with and tailored from traditional financial-services and securities standards. This is a policy statement and future legislative intention, not a Bill, enacted rule, licence condition or present obligation; the legal perimeter, transition, commencement and authorised-institution implications are not stated.

Source: LCQ1: Policy on the development of digital assets
