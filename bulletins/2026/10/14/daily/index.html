Hong Kong Regulatory Developments


🟡 Circular to licensed corporations carrying out sponsor work and engaging in bookbuilding and placing activities 🇭🇰
Item ID: item-20261013-sfc-circulars-f2ff8e07f1
⏰ Deadline: No source deadline stated
Products: IPO advisory, Bookbuilding, Placing

On 13 October, the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) issued a circular following its January communication on sponsor, bookbuilding and placing activity. It reported fewer strained sponsors and Principals, but identified uneven Principal submissions and dubious IPO arrangements. The SFC expects sponsors, overall coordinators and capital-market intermediaries to investigate, document, escalate and report concerns, and states that overall coordinators and capital-market intermediaries shall reject unresolved suspicious orders. This is a supervisory communication for the specified SFC-regulated roles, supported by underlying Code of Conduct and listing-rule duties rather than a universal banking duty. Scoped businesses may need to evidence Principal capacity, investor and funding enquiries, order-book records, escalation, reporting and rejection decisions.

Source: Circular to licensed corporations carrying out sponsor work and engaging in bookbuilding and placing activities

Material Losses, Fines & Enforcement


🟡 SFC takes action against suspected IPO-rigging syndicates and suspends dealings in Baicha shares 🇭🇰
Item ID: item-20261013-sfc-web-fdd006ef5d
⏰ Deadline: Immediate notification upon instructions concerning restricted assets
Products: Brokerage and custody, IPO distribution, Investments

On 13 October, the SFC directed the Stock Exchange of Hong Kong to suspend dealings in Baicha shares from 9:00am while it investigates suspected false or artificial IPO demand. The SFC issued restriction notices to six brokers over up to HK$4,379,463,215 and to 29 brokers over up to HK$285,030,265 in specified accounts. Recipient brokers must not deal with specified assets or execute relevant instructions without prior written SFC consent, and must notify the SFC immediately of relevant instructions. The SFC says the brokers are not subjects of its investigation, other clients are unaffected, and the investigation continues; any restitution is only a possible future process. Any recipient or operationally connected service provider may need rapid account identification, restriction configuration, instruction blocking, notification, client communication and evidence preservation.

Source: SFC takes action against suspected IPO-rigging syndicates and suspends dealings in Baicha shares

🟡 OCC Imposes $750 Million Assessment Against Bank of America for Deficient BSA/AML Compliance 🌐
Item ID: item-20261013-global-material-incidents-enforcement-cd2747adee
⏰ Deadline: US$750 million penalty payable upon execution of the OCC order
Products: Payments, Credit cards, Correspondent banking

On 13 October, the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) imposed a US$750 million civil money penalty on Bank of America, N.A. in a final consent order. The Comptroller found continuing BSA/AML-program deficiencies after a 2024 cease-and-desist order, including transaction monitoring, SAR escalation and quality, investigator capacity, audit testing, correspondent-account due diligence and wire transmittor information. Bank of America neither admitted nor denied the findings and had begun corrective action; the OCC says FinCEN is separately assessing a US$1.2 billion fine that credits the OCC penalty. This is final US enforcement and an analogous control benchmark, not a Hong Kong requirement. Comparable payments, card monitoring and correspondent-banking businesses may use it to challenge backlog, escalation, monitoring, data-lineage and independent-testing evidence without implying a Hong Kong deficiency.

Source: OCC Imposes $750 Million Assessment Against Bank of America for Deficient BSA/AML Compliance
