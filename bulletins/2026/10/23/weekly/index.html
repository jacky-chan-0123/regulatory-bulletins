📔 Regulatory Bulletin — 23 October 2026 (Weekly)

Executive Summary

• 🟡 HKMA’s revised D-SIB methodology is effective immediately and adds RTGS/FPS turnover to the substitutability assessment, making payment-data lineage and prudential reporting readiness material for banks in scope.
• 🟡 HKMA expects strengthened anti-bribery and corruption safeguards in account opening, including segregation of duties and enhanced controls for third-party introducers; no fixed implementation date is stated.
• 🟡 HSBC has announced that its HKD stablecoin will be available from launch on PayMe; in the coming days, the bank says users will be able to activate an in-app wallet for peer-to-peer and merchant payments, a market development to monitor rather than a requirement for other institutions.

Hong Kong


🟡 Gazettal of revised SPM module CA-B-2 “Systemically Important Banks” 🇭🇰
Item ID: item-20261022-hkma-brdr-2a39d0304c
⏰ Deadline: Effective 23 October 2026; 12 months from HKMA notification for a newly or increased HLA requirement
Products: Deposits, payments, lending

HKMA gazetted CA-B-2 V.3 on 23 October 2026, effective that day, replacing V.2. The statutory guideline adds RTGS/FPS turnover to deposits and customer lending as substitutability indicators in the domestic systemically important bank assessment. All licensed banks are within the regular assessment scope, while other authorised institutions may be included case by case; institutions in scope submit MA(BS)24 data. A designated D-SIB, or one with a higher HLA requirement, has 12 months from formal HKMA notification to meet that requirement. The change may affect payment-data lineage, prudential reporting, capital planning and recovery or resolution management information; the source does not announce a designation, HLA amount or reporting date for any institution.

Source: Gazettal of revised SPM module CA-B-2 “Systemically Important Banks”


🟡 Anti-bribery & corruption controls for account opening 🇭🇰
Item ID: item-20261022-hkma-circulars-0c7e79396a
⏰ Deadline: No source deadline stated
Products: Deposit accounts, digital onboarding

HKMA’s 22 October circular follows cases in which syndicates offered staff bribes to bypass AML/CFT account-opening controls. It expects layered, risk-based onboarding and proportionate customer due diligence, with segregation of duties and enhanced controls for third-party introducer cases, including due diligence, approval scrutiny and management oversight. HKMA also calls for vulnerability gap analysis, risk-based look-backs, management information and clear senior-management responsibility. The expectation may affect branch, staff-led, digital and introducer-supported onboarding; the circular states no commencement, transition, reporting or compliance date.

Source: Anti-bribery & corruption controls for account opening


🟡 HSBC RedCoin launches today 🇭🇰
Item ID: item-20261023-hk-banks-news-aefdef81f0
⏰ Deadline: No source deadline stated
Products: PayMe payments, merchant payments, stablecoin wallet

HSBC says its HKD stablecoin, RedCoin (RCHKD), will be available from launch on PayMe; in the coming days, more than 3.3 million PayMe users will be able to activate an in-app stablecoin wallet and use it for peer-to-peer and peer-to-merchant payments, including at more than 100,000 merchants. The bank reports a staff pilot of nearly 100 users covering minting, activation and live transfers. It also plans later in 2026 to add mobile-app wallet functionality, tokenised-investment subscriptions and wholesale corporate and institutional use cases. This is an official bank product announcement, not an HKMA requirement or determination for other institutions; licence identifiers and control statements are HSBC-reported. The development may affect market expectations for customer disclosures, wallet and payment operations, financial-crime controls, merchant settlement and complaint handling, while the release does not provide adoption, resilience, eligibility or full control-design evidence.

Source: HSBC RedCoin launches today
