🟡 MAS revokes Sir Money Changer’s Major Payment Institution Licence 🇸🇬
Item ID: item-20261014-mas-regulatory-announcements-c9fb3222f6
⏰ Deadline: Licence revocation effective 15 September 2026; no additional source deadline stated
Products: Payments, FX, Cross-border transfers

MAS revoked Sir Money Changer Pte. Ltd.’s Major Payment Institution Licence under the Payment Services Act 2019, effective 15 September 2026. MAS says the firm can no longer provide cross-border money-transfer or money-changing services and that its investigation found a third party independently operated a cross-border transfer business under the firm’s name at one of its places of business. MAS also found that the firm provided false or misleading information, and states that licensees must not let third parties use their licences and payment providers must maintain effective oversight and control. The final Singapore case is an analogous benchmark for third-party oversight and licence-perimeter governance, not a Hong Kong obligation.

Source: MAS Revokes the Major Payment Institution License of Sir Money Changer Pte Ltd


🟡 FCA motor-finance legal challenge set for February 2027 🇬🇧
Item ID: item-20261013-fca-benchmark-a35220ff44
⏰ Deadline: UK Upper Tribunal hearing: 16–26 February 2027; judgment expected in the following months
Products: Lending, Complaints handling, Customer remediation

The FCA says legal challenges to its motor-finance compensation scheme will be heard by the Upper Tribunal from 16 to 26 February 2027, with judgment expected in the following months. The scheme is partly suspended, but the FCA says lenders must continue preparing for provisions that remain in force and progress complaints as far as possible, including specified consumer letters. The FCA expects lenders to plan rigorously for a possible quashing, make necessary financial provisions and engage auditors; normal complaints statutory timescales could resume in that scenario. This remains an active UK legal process and a customer-remediation and operational-readiness benchmark only, not a Hong Kong requirement.

Source: Motor finance legal challenge to be heard in February 2027
Primary source: official document
