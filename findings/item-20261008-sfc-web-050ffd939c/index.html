SFC, AFRC and the Exchange join forces to address disclaimers of opinion solely relating to going concern

The SFC, AFRC and the Exchange issued a joint statement setting supervisory expectations for listed issuers with audit disclaimers solely relating to going concern. It expects robust assessments supported by reasonable assumptions, reliable data and feasible action plans, alongside detailed annual and quarterly progress disclosures and critical audit-committee and auditor review. The statement does not amend Listing Rules or set a new deadline; the Exchange may consider rule changes if improvement is not significant.

The SFC, AFRC and the Exchange have issued a joint statement on listed issuers whose audit disclaimer relates solely to going concern. It expects issuer management to use reasonable assumptions, reliable data and feasible action plans in going-concern assessments, with detailed annual and quarterly disclosure of implementation, progress, deviations and revisions. It also expects audit committees and auditors to critically challenge or evaluate the assessment and the sufficiency of disclosures. This is a supervisory expectation for listed issuers, management, audit committees and auditors—not a new general banking obligation; it does not amend Listing Rules or set a new deadline, although the Exchange may consider rule changes if improvement is not significant. Listed banking-group issuers and investment or credit teams with exposure to affected issuers may be affected through disclosure-quality, valuation, counterparty and investor-protection considerations.

SFC and AFRC extend regulatory cooperation to financial reporting of licensed firms and funds under new MoU

The SFC and AFRC signed a new MoU on 28 September 2026, replacing their February 2021 MoU. It covers financial and, where applicable, compliance reporting by licensed corporations, SFC-licensed virtual asset service providers, SFC-authorised funds and registered open-ended fund companies, and related audit and assurance work. The MoU provides for information sharing, referrals, mutual assistance and coordinated inspections or investigations in matters of mutual regulatory interest, subject to law.

GS-2 Transition Planning — corrected finding

HKMA issued GS-2 V.1 on 22 September 2026 as a non-statutory SPM guidance note applicable to all authorized institutions.
