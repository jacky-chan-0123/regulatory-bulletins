HSBC RedCoin launches today

HSBC says its HKD stablecoin, RedCoin (RCHKD), will be available from launch on PayMe. In the coming days, the bank says more than 3.3 million PayMe users will be able to activate an in-app wallet and use it for peer-to-peer and peer-to-merchant payments, including at more than 100,000 merchants; it also describes later planned mobile-app, tokenised-investment and wholesale use cases.

Products: PayMe payments, merchant payments, stablecoin wallet

HSBC says its HKD stablecoin, RedCoin (RCHKD), will be available from launch on PayMe; in the coming days, more than 3.3 million PayMe users will be able to activate an in-app stablecoin wallet and use it for peer-to-peer and peer-to-merchant payments, including at more than 100,000 merchants. The bank reports a staff pilot of nearly 100 users covering minting, activation and live transfers. It also plans later in 2026 to add mobile-app wallet functionality, tokenised-investment subscriptions and wholesale corporate and institutional use cases. This is an official bank product announcement, not an HKMA requirement or determination for other institutions; licence identifiers and control statements are HSBC-reported. The development may affect market expectations for customer disclosures, wallet and payment operations, financial-crime controls, merchant settlement and complaint handling, while the release does not provide adoption, resilience, eligibility or full control-design evidence.

Canada Gazette, Part I, Volume 160, Number 43: Stablecoin Regulations

Canada’s Department of Finance prepublished proposed Stablecoin Regulations on 24 October 2026, with related Retail Payment Activities Regulations amendments. The consultation proposes requirements for issuer registration, reserves, redemption, governance, data security, recovery and resolution, reporting and notices; representations are invited within 60 days.

UK · Financial Conduct Authority · 2026-09-16

Crypto firms get guidance on how the new regime applies

The FCA says its future UK cryptoasset-regime guidance explains which activities may require authorisation, including qualifying-stablecoin issuance, platform operation, dealing or arranging, safeguarding and arranging staking. Applications open on 30 September 2026 and the regime is due to enter force on 25 October 2027; this is a UK benchmark, not a Hong Kong requirement.
