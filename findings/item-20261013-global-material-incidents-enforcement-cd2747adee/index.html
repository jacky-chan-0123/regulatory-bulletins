OCC Imposes $750 Million Assessment Against Bank of America for Deficient BSA/AML Compliance

The OCC issued a final consent order imposing a US$750 million civil money penalty on Bank of America for continuing BSA/AML-program deficiencies, providing an analogous non-binding control benchmark for Hong Kong firms.

⏰ Deadline: US$750 million penalty payable upon execution of the OCC order
Products: Payments, Credit cards, Correspondent banking

On 13 October, the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) imposed a US$750 million civil money penalty on Bank of America, N.A. in a final consent order. The Comptroller found continuing BSA/AML-program deficiencies after a 2024 cease-and-desist order, including transaction monitoring, SAR escalation and quality, investigator capacity, audit testing, correspondent-account due diligence and wire transmittor information. Bank of America neither admitted nor denied the findings and had begun corrective action; the OCC says FinCEN is separately assessing a US$1.2 billion fine that credits the OCC penalty. This is final US enforcement and an analogous control benchmark, not a Hong Kong requirement. Comparable payments, card monitoring and correspondent-banking businesses may use it to challenge backlog, escalation, monitoring, data-lineage and independent-testing evidence without implying a Hong Kong deficiency.
