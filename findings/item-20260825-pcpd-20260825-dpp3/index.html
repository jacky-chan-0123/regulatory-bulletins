Employers' data in loan applications: PCPD findings

The Privacy Commissioner's Office found misuse of employers' personal data in loan applications and advised financial institutions to remain vigilant, without finding that a lender breached the PDPO or creating a new lender rule.

⏰ Deadline: No source deadline stated
Products: Lending, loan applications, collections

The Privacy Commissioner’s Office found that three foreign domestic helpers contravened Data Protection Principle 3(1) by using employers’ names, residential addresses and/or telephone numbers for loan applications without prescribed consent, and issued warning letters to the helpers. The report says employers received debt contacts or overdue notices after defaults; one case involved four financial institutions. This is not a finding that any financial institution contravened the PDPO, and it does not create a new lender rule or deadline. PCPD says financial institutions should remain vigilant and avoid improper use of employers’ personal data in loan applications; lending and collections owners may use the findings as a risk-based trigger to assess how third-party data, consent and contact use are recorded and evidenced.

Source: Privacy Commissioner’s Office Published a Report Relating to Three Cases Involving Misuse of Employers’ Personal Data by Foreign Domestic Helpers for Loan Applications
