Mitigating risks in receiving deposits through simplified eDDA arrangements

The SFC expects in-scope licensed corporations and virtual-asset firms using payee-initiated simplified eDDA to assess authorization, identity, fraud, red-flag and indemnity risks.

⏰ Deadline: No source deadline stated
Products: eDDA, client deposits, virtual assets

The SFC’s 20 August circular addresses licensed corporations, SFC-licensed virtual-asset service providers and associated entities receiving client deposits through payee-initiated simplified eDDA. It highlights unauthorised, erroneous and fraudulent setup or debit risks, including indemnity exposure, and expects firms to assess the arrangement, reasonably ascertain account-owner authorisation, verify identity information and respond appropriately to unmet safeguards or red flags. The circular reinforces underlying internal-control, AML/CFT and notification obligations, while its examples do not prescribe a single control design. It may affect a banking group only where an in-scope securities or virtual-asset entity uses this arrangement, rather than ordinary bank eDDA; management should confirm that perimeter and evidence the risk assessment, checks, red-flag handling and client settings or consent.

Source: Circular to licensed corporations, SFC-licensed virtual asset service providers and associated entities — Mitigating risks in receiving deposits through simplified eDDA arrangements
