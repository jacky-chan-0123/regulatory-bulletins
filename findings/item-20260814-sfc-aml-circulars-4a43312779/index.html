SFC early alert on UN sanctions-list amendments

The SFC expects in-scope securities and virtual-asset firms to update screening data following amendments to the UN ISIL and Al-Qaida sanctions list, while the corresponding Hong Kong Gazette update remained pending.

⏰ Deadline: No source deadline stated
Products: Investment services, payments, virtual assets

The Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) issued circular SFO/IS/030/2026 on 14 August after the relevant UN Security Council committee amended details for three individuals and one entity on the ISIL (Da’esh) and Al-Qaida sanctions list on 13 August, New York time. The SFC expects licensed corporations, SFC-licensed virtual asset service providers and associated entities to update customer and payment screening databases; it presents this within the expected standards in its 7 February 2018 UN-sanctions circular, not as a new bank-wide rule. The direct perimeter is the named SFC-regulated entities, with bank relevance where registered securities activity or shared screening is involved; delayed data ingestion or incomplete screening may affect client, beneficial-owner and counterparty screening. The SFC says an updated Cap. 537CB list was published on 14 August, while the corresponding Cap. 575 Gazette update was pending; it states no screening population, system design or compliance deadline.

Source: SFC circular: Early Alert on United Nations Sanctions

SFC, AFRC and the Exchange join forces to address disclaimers of opinion solely relating to going concern

The SFC, AFRC and the Exchange issued a joint statement setting supervisory expectations for listed issuers with audit disclaimers solely relating to going concern. It expects robust assessments supported by reasonable assumptions, reliable data and feasible action plans, alongside detailed annual and quarterly progress disclosures and critical audit-committee and auditor review. The statement does not amend Listing Rules or set a new deadline; the Exchange may consider rule changes if improvement is not significant.

Obtaining client consent under the Hong Kong Investor Identification Regime for the exchange-traded derivatives market (HKIDR-DM)

The SFC says relevant intermediaries will need express consent from individual clients for specified CID and BCAN collection and transfer under HKIDR-DM. It sets out identity-authentication, record and log expectations, while the proposed Code provision remains to be gazetted and its effective date is to be determined by the SFC.
