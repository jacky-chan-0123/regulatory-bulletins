FCA press release / multi-firm review · 2026-09-23

Firms crack down on money mules but need to do more

The FCA reports a completed review of 35 UK retail banks, payment institutions and e-money institutions. It reports suspected mule-account closures and multi-account cash-out patterns, and says it is working with industry and law enforcement on intelligence sharing; this is a UK benchmark, not a Hong Kong requirement.

⏰ Deadline: No source deadline stated
Products: Deposit accounts, Payments, Digital channels

The FCA’s 23 September release reports a completed review of 35 UK retail banks, building societies, challenger banks, payment institutions and e-money institutions, rather than a new rule or enforcement action. It reports that 238,396 suspected mule accounts were closed in 2025 and that fraudulent funds commonly cashed out through the second to fifth account; some accounts showed repeated suspected mule activity before closure. The FCA cautions that more closures may reflect customer growth and improved identification rather than a higher mule proportion, and says it is working with industry and law enforcement on intelligence sharing. This is an analogous UK benchmark only: comparable Hong Kong deposit-account and payment journeys may consider network detection, early intervention and lawful sharing controls, without inferring local prevalence or a Hong Kong legal basis for sharing.

Crypto firms get guidance on how the new regime applies

The FCA says its future UK cryptoasset-regime guidance explains which activities may require authorisation, including qualifying-stablecoin issuance, platform operation, dealing or arranging, safeguarding and arranging staking. Applications open on 30 September 2026 and the regime is due to enter force on 25 October 2027; this is a UK benchmark, not a Hong Kong requirement.

Policy Address signals future consultation on AI-enabled fraudulent content

The Hong Kong Government says the Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau will consult later in 2026 on expanding a legal framework to encourage technology and telecommunications sectors to improve detection and removal of potentially fraudulent online content, including AI-generated content. The announcement states no consultation date, final scope, implementation date or bank duty.

SFC circular on Haiti and Yemen sanctions amendments

The SFC reported that the Haiti and Yemen sanctions amendment regulations were gazetted on 11 September 2026 with immediate effect.
