Government welcomes passage of Inland Revenue (Amendment) (Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework and Amended Common Reporting Standard) Bill 2026

The Government announced that the Legislative Council passed the Bill to implement OECD CARF and amended CRS. Relevant service providers will collect CARF-specified information in 2027 for IRD exchanges from 2028; amended CRS takes effect in 2028 and exchanges begin in 2029. IRD will issue guidance and provide technical support.

⏰ Deadline: CARF information collection in 2027; exchanges from 2028; amended CRS takes effect in 2028 and exchanges start in 2029
Products: Digital assets, deposits, investments

The Government announced that the Legislative Council passed the Bill to implement the OECD Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF) and amended Common Reporting Standard (CRS). It states that relevant service providers will collect CARF-specified information in 2027 for Inland Revenue Department exchanges with partner jurisdictions from 2028; amended CRS takes effect in 2028, with exchanges from 2029. The announcement says the exchanges will be reciprocal and subject to data confidentiality and security, and that the IRD will issue guidance and provide technical support. The announcement does not set out covered providers or accounts, exemptions, reporting format, due-diligence rules or commencement mechanics, so applicability should remain subject to enacted text and IRD guidance.

Actions: In the next 30 days, Tax Reporting and Compliance should assign accountability, document an applicability decision, and inventory relevant entities, products and data. Then map CRS and crypto-asset data lineage, vendor dependencies and customer communications, with second-line challenge, before designing configuration or testing against operative requirements and IRD guidance.
