Policy Address signals future consultation on AI-enabled fraudulent content

The Hong Kong Government says the Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau will consult later in 2026 on expanding a legal framework to encourage technology and telecommunications sectors to improve detection and removal of potentially fraudulent online content, including AI-generated content. The announcement states no consultation date, final scope, implementation date or bank duty.

⏰ Deadline: Later in 2026 (public consultation; exact date not stated)
Products: Payments, Digital channels

The Government's 16 September Policy Address says the Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau will conduct a public consultation later in 2026 on expanding the legal framework to encourage technology and telecommunications sectors to improve detection and removal of potentially fraudulent online content, including content generated through AI misuse. The announcement gives no consultation paper, exact consultation date, final legal scope, implementation date or bank duty. It does not establish a current bank obligation or HKMA requirement; however, digital-payment customers may remain exposed to AI-enabled scam content while the policy develops. Fraud-risk and Legal/Compliance teams should log the consultation for horizon scanning and, once published, make an applicability decision covering affected payment journeys, customer harm, data/privacy and external-provider dependencies, retaining the decision and monitoring evidence.

Singapore · Monetary Authority of Singapore · 2026-10-07

MAS AI Risk Management Guidelines — omitted finding correction

MAS's official 7 October 2026 PDF announces final AI Risk Management Guidelines for all financial institutions and all forms of AI. It describes supervisory expectations for board and senior-management accountability; AI-use inventories and materiality assessment; proportionate controls across data governance, testing, human oversight, cybersecurity, monitoring and change management; and accountability and assurance for third-party AI. Existing governance may be used where adequate, and a dedicated AI committee is not inherently required. If third-party AI risks cannot be brought within risk appetite, an FI should consider limiting, suspending or replacing the service. Basic governance may be proportionate where AI failure or unavailability is unlikely to have material impact on the FI, customers or other stakeholders. Sections 3–4 take effect on 7 October 2027 and Sections 5–6 by 7 October 2028; MAS intends to consult in 2027 on possible additional agentic-AI guidance. The operative Guidelines and response-to-feedback paper remained unavailable behind MAS maintenance content, so this correction makes no unsupported subsection-level claim.
